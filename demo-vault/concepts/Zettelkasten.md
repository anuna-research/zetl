# Zettelkasten

The Zettelkasten ("slip box") is a [[Knowledge Management]] method developed by [[Niklas Luhmann]]. It emphasizes creating [[Atomic Notes]] that are densely interlinked, forming an emergent web of ideas rather than a rigid hierarchy.

## Core Principles

1. **Atomicity** — Each note captures a single idea (see [[Atomic Notes]])
2. **Connectivity** — Notes gain value through [[Bidirectional Links]]
3. **Emergence** — Structure arises organically, not top-down (see [[Emergent Structure]])
4. **Personal voice** — Notes are written in your own words to aid understanding

## How It Works

A practitioner writes a note, then asks: "What existing notes does this relate to?" By linking new notes to existing ones via [[Bidirectional Links]], a network of knowledge grows over time. This is fundamentally different from [[Folder-based Organization]].

The method pairs well with tools like [[Obsidian]] and [[Logseq]], which provide digital implementations of the slip box.

## History

Luhmann used a physical card system with numbered index cards. He produced over 90,000 cards and published more than 70 books, attributing his prolific output to the Zettelkasten. See [[Luhmann's Slip Box]] for details on his physical system.
