# Tier 2 Adversarial Review — SPEC-034 v0.4.0

## Reviewer Context

- Model: `claude-opus-4-7[1m]`
- Session: fresh; no prior exposure to the v0.3 → v0.4 iteration on this repo
- Prior SPEC-034 reviews (v0.3.0 and internal v0.4.0 self-check) were same-session per §16; this review is the first cross-context pass.
- Materials consulted: `specs/SPEC-034.md` end-to-end; prior-art references per §1.4 (conceptual knowledge, not live-fetched — see §6 of this review for caveats).

## Scope and Severity Key

Five priority areas per task brief: (a) REQ-3427+CON-3411 signing byte-range; (b) REQ-3422+ADR-3413 X25519 padding; (c) REQ-3414+ADR-3414 per-cohort PRF salt; (d) ADR-3415 TOFU residual leak; (e) CON-3409 challenge generation. I also took one full pass through the rest of the spec for defects not covered by these areas.

Severity convention (matching SPEC-032-033-REVIEW cadence):

- **S1** — blocker; claim made by the spec is contradicted, mitigation is broken, or a named attack is not actually defeated. Spec cannot move `draft → approved` without resolution.
- **S2** — material gap; spec silently relies on an assumption that needs to be stated, or mitigation is incomplete in a way that could bite implementers. Addressable in a v0.5.0 patch.
- **S3** — clarity, robustness, or completeness. Worth fixing; does not block approval.

---

## Summary

Four S1 findings, five S2 findings, five S3 findings.

The S1s are clustered around two themes:

1. **Trust-chain pinning for the shim and signing pubkey.** The spec claims Ed25519 content signing "blocks" CDN substitution (A3), but the shim — and the embedded vault-signing pubkey — ship from the same CDN. SRI is declared in the same HTML the CDN is serving. This is not "blocked"; it is "bounded once the reader has a cached shim." That reframe changes the threat-model story materially and should change at least one ADR.

2. **Envelope semantics.** The envelope headers (cohort id, mode, slug, build epoch) are explicitly carved out of the signed byte-range (CON-3404). The spec justifies this ("cleanly separable from authenticated content"), but the headers drive dispatch decisions in the shim — cohort-id selects recipients; mode selects TOFU vs PRF branch. Unsigned dispatch-controlling metadata is the classic cryptographic-doom-principle foot-gun.

The S2s are an assortment of concrete spec gaps (PRF-to-X25519 derivation is not written down; CSP `connect-src 'none'` contradicts the shim's `fetch()`; split-key mode's "spoken-phrase" second factor cannot carry 256 bits) plus honest expansions of the §11.2 residual-leak list (browser telemetry to Safe Browsing / SmartScreen, pre-CTAP2.1 PRF-on-create() compatibility).

The S3s are spec-hygiene: salt concatenation without length-prefixing, unspecified behaviour on cohort-mode switch, unspecified behaviour on `410 Gone`, virtual-authenticator extension attacks absent from §11.2, and missing post-quantum posture.

I did **not** find issues with the X25519 padding construction itself — the cryptographic indistinguishability claim checks out given the "discard private key" invariant. I also agree ADR-3415's TOFU residual-leak framing is honest as far as it goes; my quarrel is with completeness of enumerated channels, not honesty.

## S1 Findings

### S1-01 — Envelope headers are unsigned but drive shim dispatch (REQ-3427 / CON-3404)

**Affected:** CON-3404, CON-3411, REQ-3427, §2.2, §11.1 attack/mitigation matrix.

**Claim from spec:**

> "The Ed25519 signature covers the bytes of the age ciphertext only (excluding the envelope headers, which exist purely for client-side dispatch). Changing any envelope header invalidates NO signature; changing ciphertext bytes invalidates the signature. This is intentional: headers are cleanly separable from authenticated content."
> — CON-3404

**Problem.** The headers are _not_ separable from authentication; they control authentication-relevant dispatch. Specifically:

- `Zetl-Cohort-Id` — tells the shim which cohort's recipients apply. A CDN attacker can re-serve a ciphertext under a different cohort label. The age-level decryption will only succeed if the reader's key happens to be in the attacker-claimed cohort, so this does not directly yield plaintext. However, it _does_ cause UX confusion, influences observability counters (OBS-34xx stats get mis-attributed), and — more concerning — can be combined with `Zetl-Cohort-Mode`.
- `Zetl-Cohort-Mode` — tells the shim whether to take the TOFU/delegated-URL branch or the PRF-only branch. An attacker who flips `delegated-url → webauthn-prf` on a ciphertext delivered to a reader in the delegated cohort causes the shim to enter the hardened-mode branch: prompt for a passkey the reader doesn't have for this cohort, or silently fail. Flip the other way for a hardened reader and the shim will fish for `#k=` in a URL that was issued without a fragment — the page fails, and the UX hint is "your URL is wrong; ask your operator for a re-invite." This is a cheap path to a social-engineering set-up: attacker triggers confusion that drives a re-invitation request over an attacker-influenced channel. Not full compromise, but a credible phishing scaffold.
- `Zetl-Slug` — slug is used by `rewriteInWikiHrefs()` and in cross-page link targets. Mismatched slug → cross-links point at the wrong page; an attacker can cause readers to follow links into attacker-chosen ciphertexts.
- `Zetl-Build-Epoch` — can be rewound or advanced. Freshness checks, if any, become meaningless.

**The spec's own example contradicts CON-3404.** The §2.2 ASCII diagram says:

> `Zetl-Signature: <b64url-ed25519-sig over all bytes below>`

"All bytes below" would include the blank line and then the age ciphertext. But CON-3404 says only the ciphertext. These are internally inconsistent even before we get to the security argument.

**Why this is S1, not S2.** A cohort-member who is _also_ a malicious contributor (A5 + insider) can combine this with a CDN compromise (A3) to pivot. More fundamentally: the spec claims A3 is _blocked_ by Ed25519 in the §11.1 matrix. An attacker who can mutate dispatch metadata at will, and whose mutation is not rejected by the signature layer, has not been blocked in the colloquial sense — they have been forced into a narrower lane. The matrix entry is overstated.

**Recommendation.** Put all `Zetl-*` headers into the signed byte range via a canonical serialization (alphabetised keys, fixed delimiter, length-prefixed values) — or move them into the AEAD-protected plaintext payload. A reasonable minimum invariant: **every input the shim consumes from the wire format before `ageDecrypt()` must be covered by the Ed25519 signature.** Rewrite CON-3404 to that invariant; drop the "headers are separable" justification entirely.

If the authors want to keep dispatch metadata out of the AEAD for caching reasons (e.g., `Zetl-Build-Epoch` used for 304-not-modified heuristics), commit those fields to the signed byte range but allow them to be parsed before decryption — committed ≠ encrypted.

### S1-02 — "Blocks CDN substitution" claim is not supported once the shim delivery path is considered (REQ-3427 / ADR-3412)

**Affected:** ADR-3412, REQ-3427 acceptance criteria, §11.1 attack matrix entry "CDN substitutes attacker-written ciphertext".

**Claim.** ADR-3412:

> "Closes the CDN-substitution attack (A3): a compromised CDN cannot substitute attacker-written content and have the shim render it, because the attacker lacks the signing private key."

And §11.1 matrix row: `CDN substitutes attacker-written ciphertext | A3 | Blocked (NEW): Ed25519 signature verification (REQ-3427, ADR-3412)`.

**Problem.** The shim — which performs the verification — is itself served by the same CDN. Likewise:

- The HTML shell at `/c/<path-cap>/<slug>.html` ships from the CDN and contains `<script src="/assets/shim.js" integrity="sha384-<hash>">`.
- The `integrity="..."` SRI hash is in the CDN-served HTML.
- The vault-signing pubkey is embedded inside `/assets/shim.js`, also CDN-served.

On a reader's **first** visit (or first visit after the HTML cache expires), a compromised CDN controls all three of {HTML, shim bundle, SRI hash}. The CDN can:

1. Serve HTML with a modified SRI hash pointing at a modified shim bundle.
2. The modified shim bundle embeds an attacker-chosen vault-signing pubkey.
3. Any ciphertext the attacker signs with the matching attacker private key verifies against the embedded (attacker-chosen) pubkey.

The SRI check succeeds — the CDN declared the hash and served bytes matching it. The Ed25519 verification succeeds — against the attacker's pubkey. The reader renders attacker plaintext.

On subsequent visits, if the browser has cached the shim bundle _and the HTML_, the cached SRI prevents swap. But HTML is not `Cache-Control: immutable` (it cannot be — content rotates) and is fetched on each visit. The shim may be cached (`Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` per CON-3406) but only if its filename-hash URL hasn't changed; the HTML controls _which_ shim URL is fetched, so the CDN can always swap to a new shim URL that bypasses the cache.

**Net:** Ed25519 content signing defends against _only_ CDN mis-substitution of ciphertext when the shim delivery path is already trusted by other means (e.g., a reader who knows via OOB channel which SHA-384 to expect for the shim). It does not defend the first visit, and it does not defend against simultaneous HTML+shim swap at any visit.

**This is not a fatal flaw** — belt-and-suspenders is still worth something; a CDN running a smash-and-grab attack without realising the content is capability-signed might substitute ciphertext without substituting the shim, in which case verification catches them. But the spec's framing as "blocked" overstates the property.

**Recommendation.**

1. Rewrite ADR-3412's rationale: "Adds ciphertext authenticity on top of TLS-to-CDN. Defends against CDN operators who substitute ciphertext but not shim. Does not defend against a CDN operator who substitutes both. See ADR-3412b."
2. Add ADR-3412b explicitly naming the trust surface for the shim: "The shim bundle and HTML shell are delivered by the CDN and are therefore trusted to the same level as the CDN's TLS termination. Operators for whom CDN compromise is a serious threat must deliver the shim via an out-of-band trust path (browser extension, user-installed stub, separate origin with cross-origin constraints)." The spec can defer _implementing_ such a path; it must not silently claim A3 is blocked when it is not.
3. Update §11.1 row to `Partially mitigated: Ed25519 blocks ciphertext-only substitution; first-visit and HTML-cache-miss visits remain trust-CDN. See ADR-3412b.`
4. Consider recommending Certificate Transparency monitoring for the CDN + HSTS preload as operator-side controls; mention is free, implementation is operator's problem.

**Why S1.** The §11.1 matrix is the spec's security-posture contract. Overstating a defence in the matrix propagates directly into implementer assumptions and downstream security copy ("Ed25519 stops CDN tampering"). Future readers _will_ believe this unless it is fixed. Constitutional Principle 5 (Security by Design — threat-model honesty) is the specific principle violated.

### S1-03 — Hardened-mode X25519 identity derivation from PRF output is unspecified (REQ-3414 / HP-0-hardened / CON-3409)

**Affected:** REQ-3414, HP-0-hardened, CON-3409, §14 open-question #6 adjacent.

**Problem.** The spec specifies the PRF _salt_ (§REQ-3414) and how the prf_output is used for TOFU-wrap (CON-3409, for delegated-URL mode). For the **hardened-mode identity keypair**, the derivation is named ("cohort-scoped PRF output → X25519 identity") but not written.

Specifically, HP-0-hardened says "displays derived pubkey" without specifying:

1. **KDF composition.** Presumably HKDF-SHA-256 as the TOFU-wrap path uses it. Is it the same construction? Different `info`? Same `L=32`?
2. **`info` parameter.** Cross-purpose key separation (REQ-3414 intent, NFR-3403 KDF discipline) requires a distinct `info` for the identity derivation vs the TOFU-wrap. Candidate: `"ztl/hardened-identity/v1"`. Currently unspecified.
3. **X25519 clamping.** RFC 7748 clamping (`bits 0,1,2 of byte 0 cleared; bit 6 of byte 31 set; bit 7 of byte 31 cleared`) must be applied to the 32-byte KDF output to produce a valid X25519 private key. The spec is silent; `typage` handles this internally, but the spec should at least reference RFC 7748 and say "standard clamping applied by `age`'s X25519 recipient routines." Without this, three implementations can disagree on whether clamping is applied pre-age or by age, and produce different pubkeys.
4. **Interaction with fido2prf standard salt.** Filippo's reference implementation uses the salt `"age-encryption.org/fido2prf"`. The spec replaces this with `SHA-256("ztl/webauthn-prf/v1/" || origin || "/" || cohort_id)`. This is fine — Zetl is not trying to interoperate with fido2prf-at-rest files — but the spec should state "not intended to interoperate with fido2prf identity files" so operators don't expect that `.age` files produced by Filippo's plugin can be consumed by Zetl (or vice versa).

**Why S1.** Without an explicit derivation, two independent implementations will produce different pubkeys for the same reader+authenticator+cohort — a cross-implementation incompatibility at the very primitive Zetl is supposed to deliver. This is a classic "obvious to the author, missing from the spec" failure. A downstream implementer will write _something_; whichever arbitrary choice they land on is then load-bearing and un-documented.

**Recommendation.** Add a subsection (e.g., CON-3409b "Hardened-Mode Identity Derivation") that pins:

```
prf_salt_cohort   = SHA-256("ztl/webauthn-prf/v1/" || rp_id || "/" || cohort_id)
prf_output        = webauthn_prf_eval(prf_salt_cohort)     /* per WebAuthn L3 */
seed              = HKDF-SHA-256(
                        ikm  = prf_output,
                        salt = "",
                        info = "ztl/hardened-identity/v1",
                        L    = 32 )
priv_A_hardened   = X25519_clamp(seed)                     /* RFC 7748 §5 */
pub_A_hardened    = X25519_base_point_mul(priv_A_hardened)
```

Also resolve "origin" vs "rp_id" (see S3-01) while you are in this section.

### S1-04 — CSP `connect-src 'none'` breaks the shim's own `fetch(location.pathname)` (CON-3410 / CON-3408)

**Affected:** CON-3410, CON-3408, REQ-3421.

**Problem.** CON-3410 specifies:

```
connect-src 'none';
```

CON-3408's `renderCurrentPage` pipeline step 1 is:

```js
const envelope = await fetch(location.pathname);
```

With `connect-src 'none'`, `fetch()` is blocked — including fetches to the same origin. The shim cannot retrieve its own page envelope and the whole scheme fails closed (no page ever renders). This is not a minor CSP-tightening overshoot; it is a contradiction between two adjacent contract documents that both claim to be the source of truth.

There are two clean resolutions, both viable:

- **A.** Relax CSP to `connect-src 'self';` — the shim fetches the envelope over HTTP. This is the minimum-change fix and does not widen the attack surface meaningfully (same-origin only).
- **B.** Embed the envelope inline in the HTML shell (e.g., `<template id="ztl-envelope">...</template>` containing the base64-encoded envelope), so the shim reads from the DOM, no fetch needed. Then `connect-src 'none'` stands. This has a latency benefit (one fewer round-trip) but complicates the build artifact layout.

**Why S1.** Any competent Playwright test suite hits this immediately, but SPEC-034 is going to be reviewed against and implemented from — as-written, it specifies a system that cannot boot.

**Recommendation.** Pick A or B in a short addendum to CON-3410 and CON-3408, and align the text. I recommend B (embed-in-HTML) since it also reduces the TLS-to-CDN trust surface slightly (one fewer request whose response is trusted). But A is fine and simpler.

## S2 Findings

### S2-01 — PRF-on-`create()` assumption breaks pre-CTAP2.1 authenticators (CON-3409 / REQ-3412)

**Affected:** CON-3409, HP-0-hardened, HP-2, REQ-3412 fallback clauses.

**Problem.** CON-3409 evaluates PRF during `navigator.credentials.create()`:

```
credential = await navigator.credentials.create({
  publicKey: {
    ...
    extensions: { prf: { eval: { first: prf_salt } } }
  }
})
prf_output = credential.getClientExtensionResults().prf.results.first
```

This works on CTAP2.1+ authenticators with PRF-on-enrolment support (most platform authenticators; recent YubiKey firmware). But many deployed FIDO2 authenticators — including older Yubikey 5 firmware, older Windows Hello versions, some Android biometric implementations — support PRF only on `get()` (assertion), not `create()` (registration).

On those authenticators, `prf.results.first` returns `undefined` after `create()`. The shim would then:

- In delegated-URL mode: fail to produce `K_wrap`; cannot store priv_A; TOFU binding is broken.
- In hardened mode: fail to derive the identity pubkey; enrolment produces nothing displayable.

REQ-3412 mentions "graceful fallback for non-PRF browsers" but "non-PRF browser" is not the same as "browser with authenticator that only does PRF-on-get". The former is detectable at load time (`!('prf' in navigator.credentials)`); the latter is only detectable after `create()` returns an empty results bag.

**Recommendation.** Add to CON-3409 a two-phase fallback:

1. Try `create()` with PRF extension.
2. If `result.prf?.results?.first` is undefined, immediately call `get()` with `allowCredentials=[newCred]` and PRF extension.
3. If THAT also returns undefined, surface a clear diagnostic ("Your authenticator does not support WebAuthn PRF. Please use a compatible authenticator or fall back to delegated-URL mode.").

Also document the authenticator compatibility matrix somewhere (README or `docs/compat.md`), not in the spec itself. The spec only needs to commit to the fallback shape.

**Why not S1.** Narrow compatibility gap with a known pattern-fix. S2 because an implementer will hit it immediately in browser testing and fix it, but it should be in the spec so the fix is uniform across implementations.

### S2-02 — Browser telemetry channels (Safe Browsing, SmartScreen) not in §11.2 residual list (ADR-3415)

**Affected:** §11.2 acknowledged-residuals list, ADR-3415 rationale.

**Problem.** ADR-3415 is honest about `history.replaceState` being best-effort. The §11.2 list enumerates: browser sync, extensions, history-DB, OS-level URL capture. It does NOT enumerate:

- **Google Safe Browsing / Chrome URL reputation checks.** Chromium's Safe Browsing v4/v5 client historically hashes URL prefixes and checks locally; "Enhanced Safe Browsing" sends full URLs. Chrome with ESB enabled transmits the full URL including fragment to Google's servers for certain risk checks. This is a per-profile user setting but ships to a material fraction of Chrome users.
- **Microsoft SmartScreen.** Edge similarly checks URLs against Microsoft's reputation service. Behaviour around fragments is less well-documented but has historically included the fragment in at least some code paths.
- **In-browser translation (Chrome Translate, Safari translation).** When a page is detected as foreign-language and translated, the URL is sent to the translation backend in some flows.
- **Accessibility tools (VoiceOver, NVDA, JAWS).** Screen readers may read the URL aloud from the address bar. Not network-leaked, but "OS-level URL capture APIs" is a fair bucket — the spec lists it, which is good; but a reader using a shared device should be warned. Not separately called out.
- **Password-manager URL logging.** 1Password, Bitwarden, LastPass all log visited URLs at least briefly for autofill context. Fragment handling varies.

**Why S2.** ADR-3415's whole point is "honest documentation is better than oversold mitigation." The residual-leak list is the primary mechanism for that honesty. Missing channels undermine the goal.

**Recommendation.** Expand §11.2 first bullet ("Fragment leak during TOFU window") with an explicit taxonomy:

- Network-level: Safe Browsing (Chrome ESB), SmartScreen (Edge), translation services, DNS-over-HTTPS resolvers _if fragment leaks into hostname-field_ (it doesn't, but state this).
- Browser-local: history DB, browser sync (Chrome Sync, iCloud Tabs, Firefox Sync), tab recovery state, session restore.
- Extension-level: extensions with `tabs` / `webNavigation` permission see `location.href`.
- OS-level: accessibility services (screen readers), clipboard snapshots (iOS Universal Clipboard, macOS Handoff), system URL protocol handlers.
- Third-party: password managers, link-preview generators (Slack, Discord, Notion, Microsoft SafeLinks — already mentioned at REQ-3410).

Name the channels explicitly. "Short invite expiry" does not defend against "Google logs the URL forever"; the spec's recommendation must follow the threat.

### S2-03 — Split-key "spoken-phrase" second factor cannot carry 256 bits (REQ-3430 / ADR-3415)

**Affected:** REQ-3430, CON-3407, ADR-3415 (which motivates this option).

**Problem.** REQ-3430's XOR split requires `half2` to be the same bit-length as `priv_A` (256 bits = 32 bytes = 43 base64 characters). The config option `second_factor = "spoken-phrase"` implies verbal conveyance, but:

- A 43-character base64 string is not meaningfully speakable ("capital-K, lowercase-3, slash, ...").
- A 256-bit diceware-style mnemonic (via BIP-39 word list) is ~24 words — too long for a phone call.
- A short spoken phrase (say, 6 words from a 2048-word list = ~66 bits) cannot be a full XOR share, because if `half1` is the URL and has (256 - 66 = 190) bits of priv_A "masked" by zero-bits in half2, then an attacker who captures half1 needs only to brute-force 2^66 — trivial given specialised hardware and hours.

Reading the spec charitably: perhaps the authors intend `half2` to be a full 256-bit share, and "spoken-phrase" is a placeholder for some encoding (BIP-39?). If so, the spec needs to say that: "When `second_factor = 'spoken-phrase'`, half2 is encoded as 24 words from BIP-39 (or equivalent) and is conveyed via voice call or in-person." Even so, the UX argument breaks down — nobody is dictating 24 BIP-39 words over the phone reliably.

Reading uncharitably: the authors treat "spoken-phrase" as a short passphrase that the reader memorises. That does not XOR with a 256-bit half1.

**Recommendation.** Either:

- (A) Drop `second_factor = "spoken-phrase"` and keep only `"qr"` (camera-based full 256-bit share scan). Honest.
- (B) Keep spoken-phrase but define it explicitly as BIP-39-24-word or similar full-entropy encoding, with a UX warning that verbal transcription is error-prone.
- (C) Restructure split-key so half1 carries all but (say) 80 bits, and half2 is a short passphrase. Then explicitly cap the brute-force bound at 2^80 (marginal; not 2^128 as the rest of the spec promises).

My recommendation is (A). The QR path is a real mitigation; the spoken-phrase path as written invites operators to choose a setting that does not deliver the security it implies.

Also: REQ-3430 says "XOR-based split preserves cryptographic properties (each half is a uniform random string; neither half alone leaks any information about priv_A)." This is true IFF both halves are the same length and independent-uniform. Both conditions are violated if half2 is a short passphrase derived deterministically. Call this out.

**Why S2.** The config option exists and will be used if not removed. Operators will pick the spoken-phrase option and believe they have SPEC-034's 2^128 security property. They won't.

### S2-04 — Sanitiser denylist does not enumerate SVG `<foreignObject>`, CSS `url(data:...)`, inline `style`, `<style>` element @import (REQ-3421)

**Affected:** REQ-3421, CON-3410, `tools/sanitiser-config.toml` (referenced but not in-spec).

**Problem.** REQ-3421 deny-list bullets cover `<script>`, `<iframe>`, `<object>`, `<embed>`, `<base>`, `<meta http-equiv>`, `<link rel=preconnect|...>`, event handlers, `javascript:`/`data:`/`vbscript:` URIs in href/src, `srcdoc`/`formaction`/`ping`/`on*`, MathML `<script>`, SVG `<script>`, CSS `@import`, HTML5 `<template>`. Missing:

- **SVG `<foreignObject>`** — embeds arbitrary HTML inside SVG; if SVG is allowlisted, foreignObject can contain a `<script>` that escaped the outer denylist on some parsers.
- **SVG `<animate>` with `onbegin="..."` / `onend="..."`** — SMIL event attributes. Handled by "all event handlers" in principle; explicit call-out is defensive.
- **SVG `<use href="data:..." />`** — data URI SVG injection.
- **`<style>` element** (as opposed to `style` attribute) — inline stylesheet can contain `@import url(...)` or `background: url(javascript:...)` or `-moz-binding` in Firefox. Spec mentions "CSS @import" but not _where_ @import is banned; must be in both `<style>` elements and `<link rel=stylesheet>` contexts (`<link>` is implicitly denied by default-src but should be explicit).
- **`style` attribute** — inline styles can include `background: url(data:...)`. Allowing inline styles with URL values is risky; ammonia's default allows `style` attribute in limited contexts. The spec should pin: "inline `style` attribute: deny `url(...)` values, allow only property:value pairs from a whitelisted property-list."
- **`<form>` elements** — CSP `form-action 'none'` stops submission, but a `<form>` itself can be styled to phish; user clicks a lookalike button. Probably fine given `form-action 'none'`, but worth confirming behaviour.
- **`<meta refresh>`** — not covered by `<meta http-equiv>` deny because http-equiv values vary. Explicit call-out.
- **`<portal>`** — nascent browser feature; deny proactively.
- **Shadow DOM via custom elements** — CommonMark doesn't emit these, but a user pasting HTML... not a real concern for markdown sources, but spec says "CommonMark-generated elements preserved" which doesn't preclude extensions emitting arbitrary HTML (SPEC-032 ecosystem hooks can produce any HTML).

**Recommendation.** Either inline the full denylist into REQ-3421 (long but authoritative), or — more realistically — commit `tools/sanitiser-config.toml` as part of the spec's referenced artifacts and gate its content in the audit-diff corpus (REQ-3424). The corpus becomes the source of truth for what's blocked; the spec names the corpus.

**Why S2.** A sanitiser's strength is its test corpus, not its spec. The spec can defer to `tools/sanitiser-config.toml` + `tools/audit-diff-corpus/` — but it must say so explicitly and commit to running the corpus against the config.

### S2-05 — Signing-key rotation race window is unspecified (REQ-3427 / CON-3411 / §11.3)

**Affected:** CON-3411 signing-key rotation paragraph, §11.3 incident-response row, §14 open-question #5.

**Problem.** CON-3411 says:

> "Old shim bundle MUST be cache-invalidated at the CDN before the new signed pages roll out; otherwise readers with a cached old shim will reject new ciphertexts."

But the mechanism by which this is achieved, and the reader-visible behaviour during the transition, is not pinned down.

Concretely:

- Readers with cached (old shim, old HTML pointing at old shim URL, old ciphertext) — OK, everything verifies against old signing pubkey.
- Readers with cached (old shim) + fresh (HTML pointing at NEW shim URL, new ciphertext) — cannot happen if shim URLs are content-hashed; browser fetches new shim on seeing new URL. ✓
- Readers with fresh HTML + new shim + cached (OLD ciphertext) — new shim verifies against new pubkey, ciphertext signed with old key → verify fails → error page.
- Operators must therefore roll out atomically: new shim, new HTML, new ciphertexts all deployed before any reader visits, and all CDN caches purged simultaneously. This is operationally difficult.
- During rotation, some readers will hit stale ciphertexts. They see "signature failed" with no context. OBS-3413 fires spuriously.

**Recommendation.** Add to CON-3411 a rotation-sequence requirement:

1. Keep old signing pubkey in the new shim bundle as a "previous-key" slot, for a transition window of (say) 24 hours.
2. New shim verifies against NEW key first; if that fails, try OLD key; if either succeeds, render.
3. After transition window, next build drops the previous-key slot.

This is dual-pubkey shim verification, analogous to DNSSEC key-rollover. It's not free (doubles verification work on mismatch cases), but it closes the rotation race without operator-ballet.

Alternatively, commit to operator-ballet and write the sequence out:

1. `ztl cap rotate-signing-key --stage=1` → emits new shim bundle, new HTML pointing at new shim URL, but still signs ciphertexts with OLD key.
2. Operator deploys.
3. `ztl cap rotate-signing-key --stage=2` → starts signing with new key.
4. Operator deploys again.
5. Operator purges CDN cache.

This is messy but workable. Either is better than the current wave-of-the-hand.

**Why S2.** §14 open-question #5 explicitly flags this. Elevating the question to a spec requirement (either dual-pubkey or sequenced-deploy) is the next iteration's work. S2 because operators _can_ survive by coordinating manually, but the spec should either commit to a protocol or document the coordination cost.

## S3 Findings

### S3-01 — PRF salt concatenation is not length-prefixed; "origin" is ambiguous (REQ-3414)

**Problem.** `prf_salt = SHA-256("ztl/webauthn-prf/v1/" || origin || "/" || cohort_id)` concatenates strings with a `/` separator. If `cohort_id` contains `/` (permitted? unspecified), two different `{origin, cohort_id}` pairs can collide:

- `origin = "x.com"`, `cohort_id = "foo/bar"` → `"ztl/webauthn-prf/v1/x.com/foo/bar"`
- `origin = "x.com/foo"`, `cohort_id = "bar"` → `"ztl/webauthn-prf/v1/x.com/foo/bar"`

Origin does not usually contain `/` (it's scheme://host:port), but WebAuthn's `rp.id` is hostname-only. The spec inconsistently says "origin" and "origin_host" (CON-3409 uses `origin_host` in `rp: { id: origin_host, ...}` but `origin` in the salt). Pick one and define it.

**Recommendation.** Add length prefixes or reject non-alphanumeric `cohort_id`:

```
prf_salt = SHA-256(
    "ztl/webauthn-prf/v1" ||
    u32_be(len(rp_id))      || rp_id ||
    u32_be(len(cohort_id))  || cohort_id
)
```

Or — cheaper — constrain `cohort_id` in REQ-3404 to `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]*` (the spec already treats cohort IDs as identifiers, so this is cheap).

Also: pin `rp_id` vs `origin`. Use `rp_id` (the hostname the passkey binds to) consistently. WebAuthn passkeys are bound to `rp_id`, not origin-with-scheme-and-port, so using `rp_id` matches the security boundary.

---

### S3-02 — Virtual-authenticator extension attack is not in §11.2 (CON-3409)

**Problem.** A malicious browser extension can provide a virtual WebAuthn authenticator. On TOFU binding, the shim accepts whatever authenticator the browser surfaces. Without attestation verification (which the spec deliberately omits — fair), there is no distinguisher between the reader's real device and the extension-provided virtual authenticator.

If the extension captures the PRF output (by implementing the authenticator itself), it has the wrapping key for priv_A and can decrypt forever.

**Recommendation.** Add to §11.2:

> "Malicious browser extension as virtual authenticator: an extension with WebAuthn authenticator permissions can provide a virtual authenticator during TOFU. The shim cannot distinguish a virtual authenticator from a hardware one (attestation is not verified in capability-URL mode). Readers on shared or compromised machines should treat the first TOFU binding as trust-transitive on extension hygiene. No ztl-implementable mitigation; documented for operator/reader awareness."

Not S2 because the threat requires pre-existing extension-install compromise, which is a broad browser-level concern, not SPEC-034 specific. But omitting it from the residuals list is an inconsistency with §11.2's general posture.

---

### S3-03 — Cohort-mode switch behaviour is unspecified (REQ-3404 / CON-3402)

**Problem.** If a cohort's `mode` changes from `delegated-url` to `webauthn-prf` (or vice versa) between two builds:

- Existing readers in delegated-URL mode have `priv_A` bound in IndexedDB. New ciphertexts are encrypted to PRF-derived recipients. Reader's `priv_A` is not in the recipients list → decryption fails.
- The shim enters the hardened-mode branch (dispatching on `Zetl-Cohort-Mode` — see S1-01 for why this is itself load-bearing) and asks the reader to enrol. Reader enrols; new pubkey goes to the operator; operator rebuilds.

This is workable _if_ documented. It is not documented.

**Recommendation.** Add a clause to REQ-3404: "Cohort mode is intended to be stable over the cohort's lifetime. Operators who change mode MUST communicate out-of-band; existing bindings are invalidated by mode change and readers must re-enrol via the new mode's flow."

---

### S3-04 — `410 Gone` response handling by the shim is unspecified (REQ-3407)

**Problem.** Deploy config includes `410 Gone` for removed pages. A `410` response has no envelope body, no ciphertext, no signature. The shim's `fetch(location.pathname)` will get a 410; what does it render?

Current spec: silent. Implementer guess: render an error. What kind? "Page not found" vs "Page was removed" vs "Signature failed" — all materially different user-facing messages.

**Recommendation.** Add to REQ-3407 or CON-3408: "On HTTP 410, the shim renders a specific `removed` message. On HTTP 404, renders `not-found`. Signature verification is not attempted on either. OBS counters differ."

---

### S3-05 — Post-quantum posture is unmentioned (§1.3)

**Problem.** Ed25519 and X25519 are not post-quantum secure. For a spec targeting encrypted wikis with long-lived content, harvest-now-decrypt-later is a real concern by 2035. The non-goals list does not mention PQ.

**Recommendation.** Add to §1.3: "Post-quantum security is not a goal. Contents encrypted today remain encryption-strength bounded by classical cryptanalysis of X25519; operators with long-term confidentiality requirements should be aware of harvest-now-decrypt-later risk. A PQ-hybrid AEAD recipient (e.g., ML-KEM + X25519) is deferred; v2 of the wire format may adopt `age-plugin-pq` or similar once standardised."

Not S2 because no 2026-era spec is expected to commit to PQ; but the non-goals list should be complete.

## Not-a-Finding Notes (positive)

Items I scrutinised and found adequate:

- **X25519 padding cryptographic claim.** The "discard private key" invariant, combined with `crypto.getRandomValues`-seeded keypair generation, does produce recipient entries structurally indistinguishable from real ones to outsiders. Insider's "tier − 1" bound (ADR-3413 acknowledged limit) is honestly stated and correct.
- **Per-cohort PRF salt (REQ-3414) addresses cross-cohort linkage.** Different salt → different HMAC-PRF output → different derived pubkey. The scheme works; my S1-03 gap is about pinning down the derivation downstream of the PRF output, not about the PRF salt itself.
- **Forward-secrecy non-goal (NFR-3414).** Explicit, unambiguous, and pointed at SPEC-020 as the recourse. Good.
- **Delegated-URL per-grant keypair (ADR-3401).** Each grant is a fresh keypair, so cross-cohort linkage by recipient pubkey is naturally mitigated in delegated-URL mode. ✓.
- **`navigator.locks` for concurrency (REQ-3429).** Correct primitive; the BroadcastChannel fallback is handwaved at spec level but that's acceptable given the spec's "very old browsers" caveat.
- **`Clear-Site-Data` on `/enroll.html` (REQ-3428).** Good defensive layering.
- **Challenge generation (CON-3409).** 32 bytes from `crypto.getRandomValues` is correct. The "no server to verify" caveat is honestly stated. My S3-02 (virtual authenticator) is adjacent but distinct.
- **Ammonia as sanitiser choice (REQ-3421).** Good choice — battle-tested; OWASP-aligned defaults.
- **Audit-diff with malicious corpus (REQ-3424).** Right approach for catching malicious-PR → XSS-in-ciphertext. The CI gate against the corpus is the key discipline.
- **`grants.toml` as git-committable (REQ-3423).** Reasonable operational choice, with noted caveat that reader names are then public (→ operators with anonymity concerns must `.gitignore` it; a one-line addition to the spec would suffice).
